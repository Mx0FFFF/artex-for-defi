# Flash liquidity, oracles, lending, liquidations, and ordering

Use this reference only for mechanisms actually present in the scoped protocol. Flash loans are a financing primitive, not a vulnerability by themselves: [Aave's flash-loan flow](https://www.aave.com/docs/aave-v3/guides/flash-loans) requires the borrowed amount plus fee to be returned in the same transaction (or an authorized debt position under the multi-asset route). A local reproduction must identify the exact price/state dependency, show the victim operation, unwind the temporary position where relevant, repay principal plus fee, and measure attacker profit and protocol/user loss. Pin chain, block, source revision, oracle configuration, liquidity, and market parameters. Never broadcast a test transaction to a live chain.

## 1. Flash-funded, same-transaction price manipulation

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** A protocol values collateral, an LP token, or a swap using a spot pool state that the attacker can move with temporary liquidity. The distorted valuation lets the attacker borrow, mint, redeem, or liquidate at an incorrect rate before pool state is restored. Inverse Finance's June 2022 incident is a concrete example: a flash-borrowed WBTC trade moved the perceived value of a Curve/Yearn collateral token and enabled DOLA borrowing. [Inverse Finance incident record](https://docs.inverse.finance/inverse-finance/inverse-finance/legacy-products/frontier-anchor)
- **Local test:** On a fixed-block fork, record independent/reference price, victim oracle output, pool reserves, collateral value, account debt, and available liquidity. Execute fund → move market → victim action → unwind → repay in one test; compare the final debt and recoverable collateral at the independent price. Assert no borrow or redemption creates unbacked debt, and calculate net profit after all swap and flash fees.
- **Disqualifiers:** No exploitable victim action at the manipulated observation point; protocol uses a price unaffected within that transaction; liquidity, caps, LTV, slippage, or fees make extraction impossible. A transient price discrepancy alone is an observation, not a finding.

## 2. TWAP sampling and multi-block manipulation

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** An oracle accumulates a pool price over time; short, stale, or incorrectly sampled windows can admit manipulated observations. A flash loan alone generally cannot alter a correctly implemented multi-block TWAP for an entire window. Uniswap's v2 design explains cumulative end-of-block sampling; Uniswap Labs' v3 analysis models consecutive-block manipulation and its cost under proof of stake. [Uniswap v2 whitepaper](https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf), [Uniswap v3 PoS oracle analysis](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswap-v3-oracles)
- **Local test:** Identify the exact observation function, update timing, window length, liquidity across ticks/reserves, and who can call `update`. Move the pool in one transaction and across the minimum number of blocks required by the code; advance block/time in the local test to match the actual sampling semantics. Compare oracle output, victim decision, and manipulation cost to extractable value. Include a control with the observation window or liquidity restored.
- **Disqualifiers:** A same-block fork test that only changes spot reserves while the consumed TWAP remains unchanged; an economically infeasible manipulation under the target's real liquidity and borrowing caps. Do not label all TWAPs unsafe or all TWAPs safe.

## 3. Feed freshness, scale, sign, and L2 sequencer state

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** A consumer accepts stale or invalid `latestRoundData`, mishandles feed/token decimals or inverted pairs, or acts on L2 prices while the sequencer is unavailable or immediately after recovery. Chainlink documents `updatedAt` for freshness, feed decimals, and L2 sequencer checks with a grace period. [Chainlink Data Feeds API](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/api-reference), [Chainlink using data feeds](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/using-data-feeds), [Chainlink L2 feed overview](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/overview)
- **Local test:** Mock a valid fresh answer, stale answer, zero/negative answer, changed feed decimals, and sequencer down/recovery states; run each price-sensitive path: borrow, withdraw, liquidation, mint/redeem. Assert rejection or conservative valuation for unusable data and correct scaling for mixed token/feed decimals. Compare with the exact feed configuration used by the deployment.
- **Disqualifiers:** `updatedAt` age without checking the feed's heartbeat/deviation policy and the application's explicit risk policy; a theoretical decimal mismatch when the deployment has a fixed, correctly normalized pair. A price feed address alone does not establish correct integration.

## 4. Flash-loan callback and repayment trust

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** An ERC-3156 borrower trusts callback arguments or `data` without verifying the lender and initiator; a lender fails to check the callback return or collect principal plus fee. The standard explicitly treats callback arguments as untrusted until the caller is verified and describes approval/repayment risks. [ERC-3156 specification and security considerations](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3156)
- **Local test:** Call `onFlashLoan` from an untrusted contract with forged initiator, token, amount, fee, and data; attempt to trigger every privileged callback branch or consume a standing allowance. Separately use a receiver that returns a bad magic value, underpays, or withholds approval. Assert the borrower cannot perform an unauthorized state change and the lender's balance increases by the specified fee after a successful loan.
- **Disqualifiers:** Presence of an ERC-3156 interface alone; a callback reachable by arbitrary callers that has no consequential action. Prove an unauthorized action or lender accounting loss with actual state deltas.

## 5. Flash-liquidity-dependent rate or risk snapshots

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** A protocol snapshots a borrow rate, collateral ratio, governance weight, or utilization while temporary liquidity is present, then retains the favorable value after the flash position is removed. ERC-3156's security discussion gives a fixed-interest-rate example in which a flash mint suppresses the rate before a long-lived borrow. [ERC-3156 security considerations](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-3156)
- **Local test:** Take baseline rate/risk state, inject temporary supply or balance, open the long-lived position, remove temporary liquidity, and repay the flash loan. Advance time/blocks locally and compare accrued debt or required collateral against an otherwise identical position opened without temporary capital. Include rate floors, rebalancing, caps, and loan fee in the control.
- **Disqualifiers:** The rate or weight recomputes after liquidity removal; short-lived distortion has no durable effect; the protocol deliberately permits the tested rate path with no safety consequence.

## 6. Borrowing power and liquidation boundary errors

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** Collateral/debt valuation, interest accrual, liquidation threshold, close factor, bonus, or token decimal conversion is inconsistent between borrow/withdraw and liquidation. Aave describes health factor and version-dependent partial/full liquidation rules; Compound v2 documents collateral factors, shortfall, close factor, and liquidation incentive. [Aave health factor and liquidation](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations), [Compound v2 Comptroller](https://docs.compound.finance/v2/comptroller/)
- **Local test:** Construct positions immediately above and below each threshold using actual deployment parameters. Exercise deposit, accrue, borrow, withdraw, repay, partial liquidation, full liquidation, and dust transitions. Assert that an account cannot borrow/withdraw into an unliquidatable shortfall; liquidation requires eligibility, respects per-market close factor/bonus and never seizes more collateral than allowed. Reconcile total debt, reserves, collateral, and liquidator balances before/after.
- **Disqualifiers:** Importing one protocol version's numeric thresholds into another; treating expected bad debt after a severe market move as a code defect without showing a violated advertised invariant. Use the target's own risk configuration and intended liquidation semantics.

## 7. Order dependence, sandwiching, and liquidation MEV

- **Mechanism and preconditions:** A public trade, mint, withdrawal, oracle update, or liquidation yields materially different user/protocol outcomes when an adversary can place transactions before and after it. Ethereum's MEV documentation describes DEX sandwiches and liquidation competition; the original Flash Boys 2.0 paper analyzes transaction reordering in DEXs. [Ethereum MEV documentation](https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/mev/), [Flash Boys 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05234)
- **Local test:** On a local fork, replay fair ordering and adversarial pre → victim → post ordering at the same starting block/state. Check `minOut`/`maxIn`, deadline, slippage, and oracle update boundaries; quantify victim loss and attacker profit net fees. For liquidations, compare eligibility and seized collateral when oracle updates and liquidations are reordered.
- **Disqualifiers:** Ordinary price impact or permissionless liquidation is not automatically a protocol vulnerability. Report only when the implementation breaks an explicit minimum-output, freshness, execution, accounting, or fairness guarantee; otherwise record the exposure as protocol/economic risk.

## Evidence standard

For any row, retain the exact source lines, config values, fixed fork block, test command, test assertions, and before/after balances or debt. Include a negative control that removes the exploit precondition. If the attack needs several blocks, model the required proposer/order control and attack cost; a one-transaction fork cannot establish multi-block feasibility. A detector warning, simulated oracle value, or profitable trade without a victim-side invariant violation remains a hypothesis.
